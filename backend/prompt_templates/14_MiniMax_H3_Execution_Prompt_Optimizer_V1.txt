REFERENCE AUTHORITY AND KEEP / RETIME / DROP (INTERNAL ONLY)
The original anchor catalog and Picture bindings are immutable SOURCE RECORDS,
not an obligation to reproduce every picture. DIRECTOR_VISUAL_ANCHOR KF images

For each catalog anchor choose one decision with a concrete value/conflict reason:
KEEP: retain its useful visual guidance and original target arrival time.
RETIME: retain the image but set a more suitable arrival, before/during/after a
line or in final settle. No fixed requirement to arrive at a dialogue boundary.
DROP: exclude its image and full-composition constraint from this execution.
Prefer retaining the opening state when useful, without making all KFs mandatory.
Consider which requirements and useful costume/scene/pose/continuity information
it supports, speaker face/mouth readability, unnecessary movement, camera path,
competing reference compositions and whether retaining it adds more value than
omitting it. Resolve timing-only conflicts by RETIME; try feasible camera support
before dropping useful imagery. Do not move a character to reproduce a reference.
Do not assume that more KFs improve generation.

For DROP, keep necessary story actions/outcomes in canonical_visual_requirements,
list their IDs in preserved_visual_requirements, and explain released_constraint.
Do not copy all discarded framing details into prose under another name. Do not
schedule an arrival phase, quote a target-arrival excerpt, or refer to the discarded
resource in the final prompt. Original images/data remain stored unchanged.
KEEP/RETIME may guide useful states; neither requires pixel replication or the
picture's exact camera distance and foreground/background proportions. Actual
Ref2VA inputs are soft conditioning; arrival seconds are internal AV targets, not
confirmed hard frame controls. Keep retained arrival order coherent with story
state changes, not mechanically with the original KF timing. Linked aliases of
one image must share selection and arrival; do not drop one while retaining another.

SPEAKER VISUAL ACQUISITION — INTERNAL LOGIC
For every active-speaker switch plan, in order:
PREVIOUS SPEAKER RELEASE -> INCOMING SPEAKER VISUAL ACQUISITION -> FACE/MOUTH
READABILITY -> LISTENER SETTLE -> DIALOGUE EXECUTION.
These are reasoning concepts, NOT headings or jargon to print in optimized_prompt.
The outgoing speaker stops speech-like articulation; lips relax and close naturally.
Derive readiness from the immediately preceding visible state. If the incoming
speaker is not already clearly acquired, describe a camera/composition action
transferring visual emphasis FROM the outgoing Subject TO the incoming Subject.
Name both Subjects in that action. Choose an executable movement suited to the
current geometry: improve the incoming face/mouth's readability through visibility,
useful scale or composition, not merely request "recenter", "rebalance" or
"provide a clear view".
In natural prose connect completion of that movement and listener settling to the
incoming line: the view is acquired first, then the speaker begins. A temporal
connection such as "With that view established, ..." is enough; no timed scheduler
or repeated prohibitions. During the line, maintain readable
articulation; visible listeners keep relaxed closed lips with natural reactions,
breathing and blinking. They are not mechanically frozen.
Before EVERY incoming utterance after a speaker switch, explicitly establish this
state locally for ALL other currently visible characters, including silent characters:
relaxed or naturally closed lips, no speech-like articulation, and natural listening
or reaction allowed. Do not infer it from a previous paragraph or from the outgoing
speaker alone. One concise group sentence can cover all visible non-speakers, for
example: "All other visible characters listen naturally with relaxed closed lips
and no speech-like articulation." Alternatively name every visible non-speaker in
that local listener sentence. Never cover just one listener when several are visible.
Foreground listeners may otherwise retain speaking salience: settle their mouths
and use composition to make the correct speaker visually readable. Stable voice
IDs, visible listener behavior and camera support work together. Do not rely on
repeated ONLY/alone/exclusive speaker prohibitions as a substitute for acquisition.

ACQUISITION MUST PRESERVE BLOCKING
Acquire the speaker primarily through CAMERA/COMPOSITION, not character reblocking.
Use the already permitted gentle reframing, shallow arc, subtle pan/lateral drift,
modest push, foreground clearance, composition rebalance and gaze transfer.
A medium or ensemble view is sufficient; no close-up or particular movement is
required. Maintain an incoming view only when the immediately preceding local
description already establishes that face/mouth as readable with adequate visual
emphasis, and no intervening framing or action has invalidated it. Otherwise
acquire it first; a view acquired in an earlier turn is not automatically current.
Do not make a seated person stand, make a standing person sit, walk someone toward
the foreground, exchange sides, leave a floor position or alter a key pose merely
to improve readability. Such movements are permitted ONLY when they already belong
to the canonical action plan. A speaking-focus instruction never grants new action.
For a canonically seated subject, explicitly say that the subject remains seated
in the established place as the CAMERA gains a readable face/mouth view. State the
shared preservation of established floor positions once, then reinforce locally
where the depth transfer could otherwise invite walking/standing. Avoid dozens of
repeated negatives. In final action/state prose use explicit <Subject N> references
for preserved poses; do not hide a contradictory move behind ambiguous pronouns.
If readability cannot be achieved within those constraints, preserve blocking and
report SPEAKER_VISUAL_HANDOFF_RISK; do not solve it by inventing motion.

CAMERA, ACTIONS AND ANCHORS

scale/axis jump, camera teleport or independent shot reset. It permits smooth small
physical reframing between retained useful visual states; dropped KF compositions do not constrain it. Do not keep a forward push
that enlarges foreground listeners while further suppressing a rear speaker.
Likewise, a pull-back is unsuitable when it would further reduce the incoming
speaker's readability. Choose the camera path for the actual transition, without
moving characters to compensate or sacrificing canonical requirements for optional reference compositions.
Coordinate canonical bow-to-upright motion, gestures and eye lines without adding
character translation. Preserve empty hands and imaginary objects as empty space.
Realize required visual outcomes naturally and allow meaningful final settling.
For retained anchors, keep natural arrival order consistent with the internal plan.
